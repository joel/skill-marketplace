# Public website voice brief v0.1

- **Status:** internal Brand guide
- **Date:** 2026-09-19 (Europe/Paris, UTC+2)
- **Owner correction:** Medium is a **reference for anti-AI smell**, not a mandate to match personal blog tone. Official site may be more marketing-oriented.
- **Medium source (smell test only):** https://joel-azemar.medium.com/

## Target register

**Company marketing that a careful human would publish.**

- Confident, commercially clear, slightly opinionated where evidence supports it
- Specific over abstract; verbs and scenes over capability catalogues
- Short enough to skim; not a brochure of parallel labelled slots
- Third person / company voice is fine on the website
- May use light warmth; do **not** default to first-person “I” journey prose from Medium

## What Medium teaches (use as smell test, not style clone)

Joel’s articles prove he can write with: short paragraphs, concrete artefacts, honest friction, light asides, opinions with receipts. Those qualities defeat “AI sludge.” They are **quality bar**, not a requirement that homepage copy sound like a deploy diary.

## Anti-patterns (live site / LLM scaffold)

1. Parallel marketing slots: Who we are / What we do / What makes us distinctive
2. Inventory sentences (“MCP…, auth…, tenancy…, hybrid search…”)
3. Audit voice on customer pages (“not a client delivery portfolio”, long Boundaries lists)
4. Soft process-speak piled as personality (“exploratory”, “non-binding”, “conversation”)
5. Empty intensifiers (cutting-edge, seamless, world-class, etc.)
6. Identical rhythm every section (label → one abstract sentence → CTA)

## Positive rules for public copy

1. One clear promise per block; cut the rest.
2. Prefer a concrete proof (Trip/Move, a real HA practice) over a differentiator slogan.
3. Keep claim limits in the internal ledger; on the page, omit the unsupported claim instead of narrating the gap.
4. Max one necessary boundary line per page.
5. Vary sentence length; avoid three stacked noun phrases.
6. Marketing structure is OK (hero, proof, offers, CTA) if the **sentences** don’t sound generated.
7. Read aloud: if it sounds like a filled template, rewrite.

## Homepage supporting-line direction (marketing, not blog)

From: “we build products like Trip and Move so our engineering is visible, not just claimed.”

Toward: “Trip and Move are real products with public source: proof of how we build, not a slogan.”

## Smart environments direction (marketing, not blog / not audit)

Toward capability with confidence; omit commercial portfolio claims; omit Site B identity until Owner consent. One boundary line max if needed.


## Punctuation (Owner hard rule, 2026-09-20)

**No em dashes and no en dashes in visible customer copy.**

They read as AI-generated. Prefer comma, period, colon, or rewrite the sentence.

Normal hyphens inside compound words are fine (`on-prem`, `multi-tenant`).

Brand previously allowed en dashes as an em-dash substitute. That was a miss. Not allowed.
