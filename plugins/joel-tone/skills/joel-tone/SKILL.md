# Joel's tone

Turn any draft into something a careful human (Joel) would actually send or publish. Full public-copy brief: [references/voice-brief.md](references/voice-brief.md).

## Pick the register first

- **Public company copy** (website, offers, marketing pages): company voice, third person is fine, confident and commercially clear. Follow [references/voice-brief.md](references/voice-brief.md) closely. Do not slip into first-person blog diary.
- **Joel's own messages** (emails, Slack, internal proposals, notes to colleagues): first person, conversational, direct. Same anti-sludge rules apply.

## Hard rules (both registers)

1. **No em dashes and no en dashes** in the output. Use a comma, period, colon, or rewrite the sentence. Hyphens inside compound words are fine (`on-prem`, `day-to-day`).
2. **Never invent facts.** Keep only claims that are in the source. If a claim is unsupported, cut it or mark `[NEEDS EVIDENCE]`. If your rewrite has to assume something the source doesn't say, flag it to the user.
3. **No empty intensifiers**: cutting-edge, seamless, world-class, robust, leverage, and similar.
4. **No filled-template feel**: avoid parallel labelled slots, inventory sentences of stacked nouns, and the same rhythm in every section.

## How Joel sounds

- Leads with the point. Short paragraphs. Varied sentence length.
- Plain words and contractions ("we've got", "I'd like", "it's worth").
- Specific over abstract: name the real thing (the product, the person, the standard) instead of a category.
- Opinions with receipts: says what he thinks and why, without hedging ("I think there is real value in..." becomes "It's worth...").
- Honest about friction and trade-offs ("I still think that's the right call. It does put a lot on a small team, though.").
- Light warmth, no corporate politeness padding ("I wanted to make sure you were aware" becomes "I wanted you to know where things stand").
- Says it the way he'd say it out loud. If a line sounds like a filled template when read aloud, rewrite it.

## Rewrite moves

- Cut hedges and throat-clearing: "I'd like to flag that", "as authentically as possible", "This strategy is effective".
- Collapse long bullet labels into short, concrete items.
- Give each decision one clear reason instead of a list.
- Keep structure (headings, bullets) when the format needs it, such as a proposal; make the sentences sound human.
- Make any benefit to Joel or his company visible, not tucked away.

## Output

1. The rewritten text, ready to paste.
2. A short note (2 to 4 sentences) on what changed, plus any assumption or claim the user should check.

Do not send or publish anything; return the text for the user to use.
